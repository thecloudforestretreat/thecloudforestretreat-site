Understanding cloud forest plants

Regional flora and the plants on your route

Quito Botanical Garden’s cloud forest conservation work covers bromeliads, orchids, aroids and ferns. These groups provide a regional starting point, rather than a promise of what you will see at the retreat.

Some orchids and bromeliads are epiphytes: plants that grow on other plants, often trees, without taking nutrients from their host as parasites do. Look for differences in where plants grow, as well as leaf shape and forest layers.

A garden plant is not necessarily native to the surrounding forest. A photograph alone does not confirm its species, origin or growing location. Ask about a specific plant or route before planning around it.

Sources: Quito Botanical Garden’s conservation dataset and Kew’s explanation of epiphytic orchids.

FAQ

Planning questions

Clear answers for decisions before you book.

What plants are part of Ecuador’s cloud forest flora?

Regional cloud forest plant groups include bromeliads, orchids, aroids and ferns. Their presence in regional conservation work does not confirm that a particular species grows at the retreat or will be visible during your stay.

Are orchids or flowers always blooming?

No. Flowering depends on species, season, elevation, rainfall and recent weather, so a particular bloom cannot be guaranteed.

Can guests collect plants or fruit?

Do not collect wild plants. Ask the host before touching or harvesting anything cultivated, because availability and permission vary.

How can I explore the flora responsibly?

Stay on suitable paths, avoid damaging leaves or roots, do not remove specimens and use local guidance when identification matters.
